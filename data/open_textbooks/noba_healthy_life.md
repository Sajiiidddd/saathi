Health psychology examines connections between psychological factors and physical health — how emotions, thoughts and behaviours influence daily health practices and bodily functioning.

Stress is a pattern of physical and psychological responses in an organism after it perceives a threatening event. Both major life events and minor daily frustrations can suppress immune function and increase illness susceptibility; even small irritations like traffic or arguments can elevate blood pressure and alter stress hormones.

Five protective factors:
1. Coping strategies — problem-focused coping addresses the stressor directly (e.g., studying for the exam you are anxious about); emotion-focused coping manages the emotional response (e.g., relaxation, a walk, a film). Problem-focused strategies generally support better mental-health outcomes when the stressor is controllable.
2. Control and self-efficacy — believing you can influence situations improves health outcomes; even simple autonomy (caring for a plant) extended longevity in assisted-living studies.
3. Social relationships — social isolation carries mortality risks comparable to regular smoking; emotional, practical and advisory support protects health substantially.
4. Positive dispositions — hostility increases heart-disease risk; optimism and positive emotions enhance recovery and longevity.
5. Stress management — relaxation exercises, meditation, biofeedback, and physical exercise are effective techniques.

Health behaviours: adequate sleep (7–8 hours), regular exercise, and avoiding smoking and excessive drinking correlate with fewer illnesses and better performance.
