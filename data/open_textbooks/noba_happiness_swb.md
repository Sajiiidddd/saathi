Subjective well-being (SWB) is the scientific term for happiness and life satisfaction — thinking and feeling that your life is going well, not badly. Happiness has three distinct components: high life satisfaction (judging your life favourably by your own standards), frequent positive feelings (enjoyment, love), and infrequent negative feelings (depression, anger, worry). Each has somewhat different causes — there is no single formula for happiness.

What influences happiness — internal causes: inborn temperament, an optimistic outlook, resilience, and how we think about events. External causes: quality of social relationships, meeting basic economic needs, societal stability, and health. A certain level of income is needed to meet our needs, but beyond that threshold additional money shows diminishing returns. Materialism interferes with happiness — prioritising relationships and intrinsic values proves more beneficial than money-seeking.

Adaptation: people react strongly to major life events — positive and negative — but gradually return toward their baseline happiness level. Resilience affects how quickly people bounce back.

Benefits of being happy: improved physical health, longer lifespans, stronger social bonds, and higher productivity. Chronic happiness is not necessary — some worry and sadness serve protective functions.

Becoming happier: cultivate meaningful relationships; find purposeful work; develop resilience and realistic optimism; live within your means; balance achievement with enjoyment; regularly examine which of the three happiness dimensions needs attention in your own life.
