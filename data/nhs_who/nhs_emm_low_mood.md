Everyone feels low or down from time to time. It does not always mean something is wrong. Feeling low is common after distressing events or major life changes, but sometimes periods of low mood happen for no obvious reason. A low mood will often pass after a couple of days or weeks — and there are small, everyday changes you can make that will usually help improve your mood. If you're still feeling down or no longer get pleasure from things for most of each day, and this lasts for several weeks, you may be experiencing depression — consider talking to a GP.

## Top tips to improve your mood

- Increase helpful activity: low mood can stop us doing important or enjoyable activities. Try listing these things and doing some each day. Start with easier ones and, as you progress, your mood should improve.
- Talk to someone: trusted friends, family and colleagues, or contacting a helpline, can help us when we are struggling.
- Get better sleep: low moods can make us feel tired, and tiredness can also have a bad impact on our mood. Improving sleep and creating a better routine helps both.
- Boost your mood with music: create a playlist to listen to when feeling low — start with slower choices and gradually move to faster, uplifting songs. This can help your mood to gradually lift too.
- A little activity every day: doing little things to be more active — like taking the stairs instead of a lift, or standing up to stretch your legs when sitting for long periods — can really lift your mood.
- Try self-help CBT techniques to deal with worries and unhelpful thoughts, work through problems in new ways and build resilience.
