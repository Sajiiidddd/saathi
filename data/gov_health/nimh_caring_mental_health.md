Mental health encompasses emotional, psychological, and social well-being. It is more than the absence of mental illness — it is fundamental to overall health and quality of life. Self-care plays an important role in maintaining mental health and supporting treatment and recovery.

Self-care tips that can help manage stress, reduce illness risk, and increase energy:
- Exercise regularly: just 30 minutes of walking every day can boost your mood and improve your health. Small amounts accumulate — consistency matters more than duration.
- Eat balanced, regular meals and drink plenty of water to enhance energy and focus. Monitor how caffeine and alcohol affect your mood.
- Sleep: keep a consistent schedule with adequate sleep duration. Reduce blue-light exposure from devices before bedtime.
- Relaxation: explore meditation, muscle relaxation, or breathing exercises. Engage in enjoyable low-stress activities like music, reading, time in nature, and hobbies.
- Goal-setting: prioritise tasks and learn to decline additional responsibilities when overwhelmed. Acknowledge daily accomplishments.
- Gratitude practice: identify and remind yourself daily of things you appreciate — write them down or review them mentally.
- Positive focus: identify and challenge negative, unhelpful thoughts.
- Social connection: reach out to friends or family members who can provide emotional support and practical help.

When to seek professional help — consult a mental health professional if severe or distressing symptoms last 2 weeks or more, such as: sleep difficulties; appetite or weight changes; difficulty getting out of bed in the morning because of mood; concentration problems; loss of interest in usually enjoyable activities; inability to complete routine tasks; irritability, frustration, or restlessness. A primary care provider can refer you to a psychologist, psychiatrist, or clinical social worker.
