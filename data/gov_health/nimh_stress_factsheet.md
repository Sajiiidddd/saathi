Stress is the physical or mental response to an external cause, such as having a lot of homework or having an illness. A stressor may be a one-time event or recurring over an extended period.

Anxiety is your body's reaction to stress and can occur even if there is no current threat. When anxiety persists and interferes with daily functioning, it may signal developing mental health concerns, including sleep problems and increased risk for anxiety disorders or depression.

Stress vs anxiety: stress typically responds to external triggers, resolves when circumstances change, and can have positive or negative effects. Anxiety is generally internal, involves persistent apprehension without an immediate threat, and interferes with daily functioning. Both share symptoms: excessive worry, uneasiness, tension, headaches, body pain, elevated blood pressure, and lost sleep.

Coping strategies: keep a journal; use relaxation practices (deep breathing, visualisation, mindfulness); exercise and maintain healthy eating habits; establish consistent sleep routines; limit caffeine; challenge negative thoughts; connect with supportive friends and family.

If you are struggling to cope, or the symptoms of your stress or anxiety will not go away, it may be time to talk to a professional. Psychotherapy and medication are common treatment options, often used together.
